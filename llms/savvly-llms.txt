# Savvly Longevity Benefit

> Savvly is not insurance, not an annuity — it is a new kind of longevity-linked security: an SEC-registered closed-end fund that delivers structured milestone payouts via market participation plus longevity-pool allocation.
> Contributions go into a low-cost S&P 500 index ETF (VOO), held at U.S. Bank.
> Investors receive milestone payouts at ages 80, 85, 90, and 95 via longevity-pool mechanics.

## Key Facts
- Product type: SEC-registered closed-end fund (Investment Company Act of 1940)
- NOT insurance, NOT an annuity — a new kind of longevity-linked security
- Underlying asset: S&P 500 index ETF (VOO)
- Custodian: U.S. Bank
- Advisor: Savvly Advisor, LLC (SEC-registered investment advisor)
- Minimum contribution: $10/month (no maximum)
- Payout milestones: Age 80 (40%), Age 85 (30%), Age 90 (20%), Age 95 (10%)
- Early withdrawal: 75% of contribution + 1% per year held, capped at 100%
- Tax treatment: Intends to qualify as a Regulated Investment Company (RIC); distributions are generally taxable as ordinary income or capital gain, and in-kind ETF payouts carry capital gains; tax-deferred when held in a tax-deferred account such as an IRA or 401(k).
- Distribution channels: Employer Benefit, Financial Advisor, Benefit Broker
- Portability: Account remains portable on job change
- Health screening: Not required
- Location: Boulder, CO

## Annual Fund Operating Expenses
- 55 bps (0.55%) on common (purchased) shares; 110 bps (1.10%) on tracking (allocated) shares
- Blended annual fund operating expense typically estimated in the 55-80 bps range depending on the participant's share mix
- All Savvly return and payout illustrations are NET of Savvly's annual fund operating expenses

## How the Longevity Pool Works
When investors exit the pool before reaching a payout milestone, their unused share may be allocated to remaining investors, potentially increasing the returns available for those who stay. This mechanism creates an additional return above pure market performance — similar in concept to mortality credits in longevity-risk pooling arrangements, but structured as an SEC-registered fund.
